The Lost Word Library: A System-Agnostic One-Shot Adventure for One Evening

An original, system-agnostic tabletop role-playing one-shot in which apprentice archivists must recover the words vanishing from a magical library, written to be run in a single evening by a classroom group or a game night table.

Something is eating the words in the Grand Lantern Library. Titles are fading from spines, signposts have gone blank, and the head librarian can no longer say her own name. The players are apprentice archivists with one night to trace the missing words to their source before the library falls silent for good.

This is a complete one-shot adventure outline. It includes the premise and opening scene, a cast of six original characters with motives and sample lines, five locations (the Reading Hall, the Map Stacks, the Bindery, the Echo Gallery, and the Sealed Index), and a set of encounters built around puzzles, conversation, and quick decisions rather than combat. A closing confrontation offers several ways to resolve the mystery, including a peaceful one.

The adventure is system-agnostic. It uses plain-language difficulty notes (easy, tricky, hard) that you can map onto whatever rules your group already likes, or onto a simple roll-a-die approach described in the introduction. No prior experience with a specific game is required, though whoever runs it should read it through once beforehand.

A pacing guide breaks the session into timed scenes so the story can be finished in roughly two to three hours, with suggestions for what to trim if time runs short. Teachers will also find notes on running it with a larger group split into teams, plus an optional party-game variant in which teams compete to restore lost words through improvised clues and wordplay prompts.

The tone is curious and lightly spooky, suitable for younger and older players alike. Wordplay, reading, and cooperative problem-solving sit at the heart of the story, which makes it a natural fit for an end-of-term activity, a club meeting, or a family game night. It is an entertainment resource, not a formal lesson plan, so please adapt it to your own group and setting.

Everything here, including the characters, setting, and mechanics, is original and not drawn from any existing game or franchise.
