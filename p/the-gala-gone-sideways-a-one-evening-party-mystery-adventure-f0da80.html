The Gala Gone Sideways: A One-Evening Party Mystery Adventure

A system-agnostic party mystery for four to six players, where guests race to find a missing heirloom serving dish before a neighborhood potluck begins.

It is the afternoon of the Marlow family's annual potluck, and the great-grandmother's cut-glass serving bowl has vanished from the hall cabinet. Your group plays the guests and household members who are all arriving with a secret, a grudge, or a dish they would rather not talk about. Nothing here needs a particular rulebook. A timer, a few index cards, and something to write with are enough to run it.

The cast includes the Host, who is quietly panicking; the Late Arrival, who swears they were never near the cabinet; the Cook, whose recipe has a suspicious ingredient; the Neighbor, who has opinions about the hedge; and the Child Helper, who knows more than they admit. Each player receives a short character card with a goal, a private secret, and one question they must answer honestly if asked directly. Game leaders keep the mystery moving without needing to know the answer in advance.

Play moves through five short rounds, each set in a different part of the house: the front porch, the kitchen, the pantry, the back garden, and the hall closet. In each round, the leader reads a prompt card that reveals a clue, forces a choice, or introduces a small complication, such as a spilled drink or a knock at the door. Players trade information, make accusations, and decide whether to protect or expose their secrets. Each round takes about fifteen minutes.

A full evening's session runs about ninety minutes, including setup and a closing vote where the group decides who took the bowl and why. Setup takes about ten minutes: deal the character cards, place the five location cards around the table, and shuffle the prompt cards. The adventure includes optional twists for larger groups and a short version for younger players who want a lighter mystery.

The Gala Gone Sideways is written for home organizers who host game nights and for anyone who enjoys mystery, roleplay, or social deduction. It is meant to be read aloud, adapted, and played again with a different solution each time.
